A productive meeting starts with a shared understanding of why you are meeting. Whether speaking with an accountant, consultant, supplier, or another professional in the U.S. or Korea, prepare enough context without sending a collection of unrelated files.

## Before the meeting

1. **Define the objective.** Write down the decision you need to make or the information you need. Separate urgent questions from those that can wait.
2. **Confirm participants.** Identify who can explain the background, answer technical questions, and make decisions. Ask whether an interpreter or bilingual materials would help.
3. **Share an agenda.** List topics in priority order and ask what documents the professional needs in advance.
4. **Prepare questions.** Ask about the process, information needed, expected deliverables, fees, and next steps where relevant. Confirm the time zone and meeting format.

## Checklist

- A short description of your business and the issue.
- Relevant dates, documents, and an agenda sent through an agreed channel.
- Questions ranked by importance and the names of participants.
- Someone responsible for notes and confirming follow-up.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Do not assume everyone has the same background or that a translated term has the same meaning in every business context. Agree on a secure way to share sensitive material before sending it. Leave time to clarify terms and confirm what each party understood.
