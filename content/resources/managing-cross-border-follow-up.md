Cross-border projects often involve different time zones, languages, work schedules, and expectations. A simple shared record can help people understand what has been agreed and what remains open.

## Set up a workable process

1. **Name contacts and decision-makers.** Identify a primary contact on each side and clarify who can approve changes or answer particular questions.
2. **Choose channels and timing.** Agree where routine updates and urgent questions should go. State time zones when scheduling calls or setting dates.
3. **Keep an action log.** Record each item, its owner, the agreed date, and its status. Make the log accessible to those responsible.
4. **Confirm decisions in writing.** Summarize decisions after a call and flag anything requiring confirmation. Invite corrections, especially where interpretation or translation was involved.
5. **Review open items.** Set a regular check-in, identify blockers early, and reconfirm responsibilities when the scope or participants change.

## Checklist

- Named contacts, decision-makers, and communication channels.
- Meeting times and deadlines with the relevant time zones.
- A shared list of decisions, open questions, owners, and next steps.
- A process for clarifying translations and correcting the record.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Do not treat silence as approval or assume a message was understood as intended. Avoid vague action items without an owner or date. Keep sensitive documents in an agreed channel rather than circulating them broadly.
